About me

I am a cybersecurity specialist and Information Systems Security Manager for U.S. Army TACOM G-6, where I review RMF authorization packages for a portfolio of arsenals and depots and serve as the command’s subject matter expert for artificial intelligence. Most of my work sits where security compliance meets software delivery: the standards are published and specific, and the job is building things that meet them provably rather than approximately.

That is also how I build with language models. The pattern I keep returning to is the same one my day job enforces — the model drafts, deterministic code audits the draft against a written standard, and a human makes the call. The interesting engineering is in that second step, the part that never calls a model at all.

Resume Forge is that idea as a product: a desktop application that writes OPM-compliant federal resumes, built around a compliance scanner that never touches the API. It parses the draft, measures it against the published hiring rules, and flags every violation instantly and for free. The model writes; the rule engine keeps it honest.

This site is a working example of the same discipline. The panel under Forge runs on your own API key, stores nothing on any server, and tells you exactly where your text goes before it goes there.
