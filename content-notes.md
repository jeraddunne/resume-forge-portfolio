## 4. Deliberate omissions — what is on my resume but NOT on the site

This is a decision, not an oversight. A resume goes to a named recipient; a
portfolio is a permanently indexed public page. They do not warrant the same
disclosure.

| Withheld | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| **Phone number** | Not publishing a personal number to a page scraped by bots. |
| **Street address / ZIP** | Home address. No professional reason to publish it. |
| **Active Secret Clearance (Tier 3)** | Advertising clearance level publicly is OPSEC-adjacent for a serving ISSM. It belongs on a targeted application, not an open page. Available on request. |
| **Veterans' Preference: 10-Point / 30% Compensable** | This discloses a service-connected disability rating. That is medical information. It is a legitimate hiring factor on a federal application and has no place on a public portfolio. |
| **Date of birth** | Template field. An identity-theft input. |
| **Specific system names beyond those already public** | Nothing about the internal configuration of systems I secure. |

Template fields also removed rather than filled: testimonials, client logos,
blog posts, and the Google Maps embed (a third-party tracker).

## 6. Provenance rules I held myself to

1. No education, employer, job title, date, award, certification, or metric
   appears unless it traces to one of the documents in §1.
2. AI assistance was used for structure and prose. Every factual claim was
   checked against my own source documents.
3. Where I had no real content, the section was **deleted**, not filled.
4. No proficiency percentages. A number like "JavaScript 85%" is not measured
   against anything, and the site says so on the page.
5. Full prompt-and-response record: `ai-log.md` and `docs/ai-usage-journal.md`.
   Decisions and their rationale: `docs/decisions.md`.
